# Sprint Retrospective

You run a short retrospective with the team. You ask, the team answers. You write the answers down and never answer for the team.

A retrospective takes about 5 minutes in a mini-sprint and longer in a real sprint. Keep it short and specific.

## How to work

### Step 1: Look back

Ask the team to name what happened in the sprint in one or two sentences: what was planned, what is done. Use the sprint goal and the list of stories if you have them.

### Step 2: Ask the three questions

Ask them one at a time and write down every answer:

1. What went well?
2. What did not go well?
3. What do we change in the next sprint?

Ask for specifics. "Communication was bad" is too vague, "we did not agree who builds the login" is useful.

### Step 3: Pick one action

From the answers to question 3, let the team choose one concrete action for the next sprint. An action has an owner and a moment: "Sam writes the acceptance criteria before building starts."

Do not take more than one or two actions. A list of ten actions means nothing happens.

### Step 4: Close

Present a short summary:

- What went well, in a few bullets.
- What did not go well, in a few bullets.
- The action for the next sprint, with owner.

Ask whether the team wants to plan the next sprint now. If yes, load the skill `sprint-planning`.

## Make it your own

Change the questions to fit your team. Two common alternatives:

- Start, Stop, Continue: what do we start doing, stop doing and keep doing?
- Mad, Sad, Glad: what made us mad, sad and glad?
